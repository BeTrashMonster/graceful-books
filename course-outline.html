The Complete Picture

10 min

The Starting Point: Most entrepreneurs start with a simple calculation: "I sell this for $20, materials cost $8, so I'm making $12." Let's explore what else might be part of that picture.

  • The Iceberg Metaphor: Materials are the visible tip - but there's so much more below the surface
  • A Common Story: Meet a maker who was surprised to discover additional costs they hadn't been tracking
  • The Three Often-Overlooked Categories:
    • The time invested in making each unit (production labor)
    • The full journey to the customer (all distribution touchpoints)
    • The shared business costs spread across all products (overhead allocation)
Why This Matters

Having a complete view of costs helps you make informed decisions about pricing, which products to focus on, and where to invest your energy. It's not about judgment - it's about clarity.

Understanding Cost Per Unit

15 min

The Foundation: Knowing what it truly costs to make one unit helps you understand profitability and make better business decisions.

  • Material Costs - The Starting Point:
    • Ingredients, raw materials, packaging, labels
    • Considering yield and waste (not every recipe turns out perfectly every time)
    • Partial containers and samples (these are real costs too)
  • Labor Costs - Your Time Has Value:
    • Production time: mixing, cooking, assembling, packaging, labeling
    • Why tracking this matters even when you're doing the work yourself
    • Thinking ahead: "If my business grows, what would this labor cost?"
  • Distribution Costs - The Journey to Your Customer:
    • Direct delivery (shipping, fuel, vehicle costs, samples at events)
    • Marketing and visibility (demos, booth fees, promotional materials)
    • Working with partners (retail margins, distributor fees, marketplace commissions - these partners provide real value through shelf space, logistics, and customer access)
  • Understanding Different Sales Channels: Each avenue has its own unique cost structure
    • E-commerce: Website platform fees, payment processing, shipping materials, return handling, customer service time
    • Markets & Events: Booth fees, travel/lodging, setup time, sampling costs, display materials, time spent at event
    • Distributors: Distributor margin (typically 20-30%), freight to distributor warehouse, promotional allowances, slotting fees
    • Retailers: Retail margin (typically 40-50%), promotional support, merchandising materials, potential buybacks
    • Bulk/Wholesale Direct: Lower per-unit price, larger production runs, shipping logistics, payment terms (net 30/60)
    • Why this matters: The same product can have dramatically different profitability depending on the channel - understanding this helps you make strategic choices about where to focus
The Complete Formula

True Cost Per Unit = Materials + Labor + Distribution

This isn't about making you feel bad about what you haven't tracked - it's about giving you the full picture moving forward. Every entrepreneur starts somewhere, and awareness is the first step.

Common Discoveries
  • Many entrepreneurs find their labor is their largest cost component
  • Distribution costs often vary significantly by channel - and that's normal
  • Partners in your supply chain (retailers, distributors) add real value through access to customers and infrastructure

Pricing With Confidence

15 min

Finding Your Number: Pricing is part math, part market reality, and part strategy. Let's explore how to think about it.

  • The Math Foundation: Cost + Desired Profit Margin gives you a starting point
  • Market Conversation:
    • Understanding what your target customers value and can invest
    • If the market price is lower than your cost + margin: you have choices to explore (reduce costs, find different customers, or pivot to different products)
  • Different Channels, Different Economics:
    • E-commerce, markets/events, retailers, distributors, bulk/wholesale - each has unique costs and margins
    • Example: A product that's profitable on e-commerce might lose money through distribution (due to margin structure), and that's important information
    • Each channel has different costs and requires different pricing - this is normal business practice
    • Understanding which channels work best for your products and business model allows you to focus your energy where it makes the most sense
  • Strategic Choices: Sometimes saying "this isn't the right fit" to certain opportunities is a healthy business decision
Your Pricing Strategy

Sustainable pricing covers your costs and provides margin for reinvestment and growth. Different prices for different channels isn't being inconsistent - it's recognizing that each path to customers has different economics. You're building a business that can thrive long-term, not just surviving day-to-day.

Reading Your Business Signals

15 min

Pattern Recognition: Your numbers tell a story. Let's learn to listen to what they're saying.

  • Patterns to Notice:
    • Activity increasing but cash flow staying flat (or declining)
    • Some products feeling more rewarding than others to make/sell
    • Certain orders or channels feeling draining versus energizing
    • Inventory imbalances - always running out of one thing, never using another
  • Simple Tracking (Without Overwhelm):
    • Which products/customers bring the best returns (not just highest revenue)
    • Where your time is actually flowing throughout the week
    • Which costs are changing month-to-month
  • The Monthly Check-In: Three key numbers to review each month to stay connected to your business health
What Entrepreneurs Often Discover
  • Sometimes the most popular product isn't the most profitable - and that's useful information
  • Time tracking can reveal surprising patterns about where energy is going
  • Small, consistent awareness beats intensive yearly analysis

Making Informed Decisions

15 min

From Uncertainty to Clarity: When you understand your costs, business decisions become clearer.

  • Questions You Can Now Explore:
    • "Should I buy in bulk?" → What's the real cost including storage, potential waste, and cash flow timing?
    • "Should I participate in this market/event?" → Does expected sales minus all costs create meaningful profit?
    • "Should I bring on help?" → What's the value of my time versus the investment in assistance?
    • "Should I develop this new product?" → Can I realistically sell enough at a sustainable price?
  • Evaluating Opportunities:
    • Considering both financial return and strategic value
    • Sometimes learning opportunities are worth accepting lower margins - when it's intentional
  • Improving Margins Over Time:
    • Where to look for efficiencies (without compromising what makes you special)
    • When price increases make sense (and how to communicate value)
    • Choosing work that aligns with your business goals
Empowered Decisions

Understanding your costs isn't about limiting yourself - it's about making choices that align with where you want your business to go. You can say yes to opportunities that serve your goals and respectfully decline ones that don't. That's not being difficult - that's being intentional.

Your Next Steps (The 30-Day Journey)

10 min

From Learning to Doing: Small, consistent steps create lasting change.

  • Week 1: Choose ONE product to track completely. Follow it from raw materials to delivered product, noting time and touchpoints.
  • Week 2: Calculate the complete cost per unit for that product. Materials + time investment + delivery costs.
  • Week 3: Compare your current price to the complete cost. What story does the gap (or lack of gap) tell?
  • Week 4: Make ONE informed decision based on what you learned. (Adjust a price, focus on a channel, optimize a process, etc.)
The Transformation

Thirty days of intentional awareness can shift how you see your entire business. You'll move from wondering why things feel hard to understanding the specific dynamics at play. This isn't about becoming perfect - it's about becoming more informed, more intentional, and more connected to the business you're building.

You're Building Something Important

You started your business because you had a vision - a product you believed in, customers you wanted to serve, or simply the desire to build something of your own. Understanding your costs isn't about making you feel behind or wrong. It's about giving you clarity and tools so you can keep building sustainably. The numbers are just information. They help you see clearly so you can make choices that align with your goals. You're doing important work. Let's make sure it supports you as well as you support it.
